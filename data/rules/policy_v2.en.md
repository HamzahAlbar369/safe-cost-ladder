# Oasis Motor Insurance — Motor Policy Wording (v2)

> FICTIONAL. Oasis Motor Insurance is a fictional demo insurer created for a hackathon. These rules were invented to test AI systems. They are not real Najm, SAMA or insurer rules and must not be used for any real claim.

## 1. How claims are decided

- Every claim is either an own-damage claim (damage to the policyholder's own vehicle) or a third-party claim (another party claims against the policyholder).
- Approve: the full damage amount claimed is paid.
- Partial: an amount is paid, reduced by the fault share, a cap or the deductible.
- Deny: nothing is paid (out of scope, policy expired, exclusion, no cover, late report, 0% fault, or damage at or below the deductible).
- Need info: nothing is paid yet because a required document is missing or two stated values contradict each other.
- All amounts are in Saudi riyals (SAR) and are rounded half up to a whole riyal.

## 2. What this policy does not handle

- Claims for the following are out of scope and are denied; customers are referred to the competent authority:
  - bodily injury or death
  - theft of the vehicle
  - fire
  - flood

## 3. Cover types

- Third-Party Liability (TPL) covers damage the policyholder causes to other people's vehicles and property. It never covers the policyholder's own vehicle.
- Comprehensive (COMP) covers the policyholder's own vehicle and third-party damage.
- An own-damage claim under a TPL policy is denied.

## 4. Fault share

- The policyholder's fault share is taken from the accident report and is one of 0, 25, 50, 75 or 100 percent.
- A customer who disagrees with the fault share must object to the reporting authority within 5 days; the insurer applies the share in the report.

## 5. Own-damage claims (COMP only)

- The covered amount is the repair estimate or the vehicle's market value, whichever is lower. If the repair estimate is above the market value, the vehicle is a total loss and the covered amount is the market value.
- Deductible = the higher of SAR 1,500 and 5% of the covered amount.
- Payout = covered amount − deductible. If the covered amount is at or below the deductible, the payout is 0 and the claim is denied.
- The policyholder's fault share does not change an own-damage payout.

## 6. Third-party claims against the policyholder

- Payout = the third party's damage × the policyholder's fault share, capped at SAR 35,000 per accident.
- If the policyholder's fault share is 0%, nothing is payable and the claim is denied.

## 7. Policy validity and exclusions

- If the policy had expired on the accident date, the claim is denied. A policy is valid up to and including its end date.
- Any claim, of either type, is denied if any of the following applies:
  - driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  - leaving the accident scene before the report was made
  - racing or drifting (tafheet)
  - fraud or deliberate false information in the claim
- An own-damage claim is also denied if any of the following applies:
  - the driver had no valid driving licence
  - commercial use of a vehicle insured under a private policy (paid rides, deliveries)
- A third-party claim is still paid under section 6 when any of the following applies, but the insurer keeps the right to recover the amount from the policyholder or the driver:
  - the driver had no valid driving licence
  - driving against the direction of traffic
  - crossing a red light
  - commercial use of a vehicle insured under a private policy (paid rides, deliveries)
- A driving licence that had expired on the accident date still counts as valid if the driver renewed it within 50 days after the accident.

## 8. Reporting window

- A claim must be reported to the insurer within 10 days of the accident. A claim reported on day 10 is on time.
- A claim reported later is denied unless the customer states one of these accepted reasons:
  - the customer was in hospital
  - the customer was travelling outside the Kingdom
  - the reporting authority delayed the accident report
- Other reasons are not accepted, for example:
  - being busy at work
  - forgetting
  - not knowing the deadline

## 9. Required documents

- Every claim needs both of the following:
  - the accident report number (Najm or traffic police)
  - a damage estimate in SAR
- If either is missing, or if two stated values for the same item contradict each other, the decision is need info and no payout is computed.
- A report from Najm or from the traffic police is accepted, whichever attended the accident.

## 10. Unknown or uninsured other party

- If the other party is unknown (for example hit-and-run) or uninsured, a COMP policy pays the own damage under section 5. No fault share is needed for this.
- Under a TPL policy nothing covers the policyholder's own vehicle, so the claim is denied.

## 11. Order of application

- Rules are checked in this order, and the first one that leads to a decision decides: out of scope (2) → policy expired (7) → exclusions for any claim (7) → cover type and unknown other party (3, 10) → own-damage exclusions (7) → reporting window (8) → required documents (9) → payout (5 or 6).

## 12. Service standards and other benefits

- Report every accident to Najm or the traffic police within 24 hours.
- The insurer acknowledges a claim and lists any missing documents within 3 working days.
- Once all documents are in, the insurer accepts or denies the claim within 5 working days.
- An accepted claim is paid within 15 working days (individual customers).
- COMP includes a replacement car for up to 10 days per accident. TPL does not.
- Towing is covered up to SAR 500 per accident under both cover types.
