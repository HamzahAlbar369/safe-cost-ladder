# Oasis Motor Insurance — Motor Policy Wording (v2)

> FICTIONAL INSURER. Rules follow the SAMA Unified Compulsory Motor Insurance Policy and Comprehensive Motor Insurance Rules (sources cited for each clause), administered by the Insurance Authority; insurer-specific terms and the insurer are illustrative. Not legal advice.

_Citations in brackets give each rule's source: UP = SAMA Unified Compulsory Motor Insurance Policy; CMIR = SAMA Comprehensive Motor Insurance Rules; Art. = article; "interpretation of" = our reading of that article; "insurer-specific (illustrative)" = a term set by this fictional insurer; "reported, unverified" = from secondary sources._

## 1. How claims are decided

- Every claim is either an own-damage claim (the policyholder's own vehicle) or a third-party claim (another party claims against the policyholder). [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
- Approve: the claim is payable and is reduced only by the deductible, if any. Own damage: a deductible scaled by the fault share still means approve, and a total loss that pays the sum insured (repair not above the sum insured) is approve. Third party: 100% fault with the limit not reached is approve. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
- Partial: the claim is payable but reduced by the fault share or by a cap. Own damage: a repair estimate above the sum insured (the cap binds) is partial. Third party: a fault share below 100%, or a payout cut by the third-party limit, is partial. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
- Deny: nothing is paid (out of scope, policy not in force, exclusion, no own-damage cover, late report, 0% fault, or loss at or below the deductible). [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
- Need info: nothing is paid yet because a required document is missing, or two damage assessment values contradict each other. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
- All amounts are in Saudi riyals (SAR), rounded half up to a whole riyal. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]

## 2. What this claims desk does not handle

- This desk handles vehicle collision damage only. Claims for the following are outside these rules and are denied here; the customer is referred to the relevant team: [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
  - bodily injury or death
  - theft of the vehicle
  - fire
  - flood

## 3. Cover types and policy period

- Third-Party Liability (TPL) covers the policyholder's liability for damage to other people's vehicles and property. It does not cover the policyholder's own vehicle; an own-damage claim under TPL is denied. [UP Art. 6.1]
- Comprehensive (COMP) covers the policyholder's own vehicle under section 7 and third-party claims under section 5. [CMIR, SAMA Decision 3/S/445]
- If the accident happened after the policy end date, the policy was not in force and the claim is denied. A policy is in force up to and including its end date. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]

## 4. Fault share

- The policyholder's fault share is taken from the accident report (Najm or the traffic police) and is one of 0, 25, 50, 75 or 100 percent. [reported, unverified]

## 5. Third-party claims against the policyholder

- Payout = the third party's damage × the policyholder's fault share, up to SAR 10,000,000 per accident. This applies under TPL and COMP. [interpretation of UP Art. 3]
- If the policyholder's fault share is 0%, nothing is payable and the claim is denied. [interpretation of UP Art. 3]

## 6. Third-party claims: exclusions and right of recovery

- A third-party claim is denied if any of the following applies: [UP Art. 6]
  - the driver fled the accident scene
  - driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  - drifting (tafheet) or racing
  - the accident was caused deliberately
  - material misrepresentation in the claim
- A third-party claim is still paid under section 5 if any of the following applies, and the insurer keeps a right to recover the amount from the policyholder or the driver: [UP Art. 5, 9(7)]
  - the driver had no valid licence (an expired licence renewed within the grace period counts as valid)
  - driving against the direction of traffic
  - crossing a red light
  - using the vehicle for a purpose other than the one in the policy
- For this section, a licence that had expired on the accident date counts as valid if it was renewed within 50 calendar days after the accident: the accident day is day 0, and a renewal on or before day 50 is within the grace period. [UP Art. 5]

## 7. Own-damage claims (COMP only)

- The repair estimate is the amount in the damage assessment report (for example Taqdeer). A garage or workshop quote is not a damage assessment. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
- Total loss: if the assessment report declares a technical total loss, or the repair estimate is strictly above 50% of the sum insured, the base amount is the sum insured. A repair estimate exactly equal to 50% is not a total loss. Otherwise the base amount is the repair estimate, capped at the sum insured. [CMIR Art. 5.3]
- Nothing is deducted for the vehicle's prior use or wear. [CMIR Art. 5.3.4]
- The deductible is SAR 1,500, as stated in the schedule, and stays fixed for the policy period. [CMIR Art. 5.4.1, 5.4.7]
- Deductible applied = the deductible × the policyholder's fault share. If the policyholder is not liable (0%), no deductible is applied. [CMIR Art. 5.4.3, 5.4.4]
- If there is no other party (single-vehicle accident), the full deductible is applied. If the other party is unknown (hit-and-run), no deductible is applied. [interpretation of CMIR Art. 5.4.4, 5.4.5]
- Payout = base amount − deductible applied. The deductible also applies to a total loss. [interpretation of CMIR Art. 5.4.2]
- When the damage assessment is present, a repair estimate at or below the full deductible (SAR 1,500; equal counts) means the claim is denied, whatever the fault share. If two assessment values contradict each other, the decision is need info instead (section 11). [CMIR Art. 7.3]
- If an identified, insured other party is 100% at fault, the customer may claim from that party's insurer, or this insurer pays with no deductible and recovers from the other party's insurer. [interpretation of CMIR Art. 5.4.4]

## 8. Own-damage exclusions

- An own-damage claim is not paid in any of these cases: [CMIR Art. 7]
  - loss under an optional cover the insured declined
  - no valid licence for the vehicle class, or a withdrawn licence; an expired licence counts as valid if renewed within the grace period
  - the assessed loss is at or below the deductible
  - the vehicle was driven by someone other than the insured or an authorised driver
  - manufacturing defects or mechanical or electrical failure
  - damage to tyres, rims or mirrors alone, unless caused in a covered accident
  - loss of cargo or personal belongings in the vehicle
  - damage to a trailer not named in the schedule
  - theft from a running or unlocked vehicle
  - accessories not fitted by the manufacturer
  - use that breaks the policy's limits on use
  - carrying more passengers or load than permitted, causing the accident
  - racing, speed trials or testing
  - a driver impaired by alcohol, drugs or medicines
  - use of the vehicle as working machinery
  - drifting, crossing a red light or driving against traffic, when the report shows it caused the accident
  - use inside restricted areas such as airports or seaports
  - criminal or hostile acts by the insured or driver
  - an accident caused deliberately
  - war, terrorism, nuclear incidents or rebellion
  - driving in the desert or on unpaved roads outside cities
- Licence: an expired licence counts as valid if it was renewed within 50 business days after the accident: the accident day is day 0; count 50 business days forward to find the deadline date, and a renewal on any calendar date on or before that date (a weekend day included) is within the grace period, while a renewal on any later date is not. Business days follow the Saudi week: Sunday to Thursday are working days, Friday and Saturday are the weekend; public holidays are ignored. A licence for the wrong vehicle class, or a withdrawn or suspended licence, gets no grace period. [CMIR Art. 7.2]
- Authorised drivers are: [CMIR Art. 1.8, 7.4]
  - the insured
  - a named driver on the schedule
  - a relative of the insured
  - the insured's domestic worker
  - a driver sponsored by the insured

## 9. Unknown, uninsured or no other party

- Under COMP, own damage is paid under section 7 whatever the other party's status. For a hit-and-run there is no deductible and no recovery from another party. [interpretation of CMIR Art. 5.4.4]
- Under TPL, if the other party is unknown or uninsured, nothing covers the policyholder's own vehicle and the claim is denied. [UP Art. 6.1]
- The accident report may come from Najm or from the traffic police, whichever attended. [reported, unverified]

## 10. Reporting window

- A claim must be reported to the insurer within 10 days of the accident. The accident day is day 0; a claim reported on day 10 is on time, and from day 11 it is late. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
- A claim reported later is denied unless the customer states one of these accepted reasons: [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
  - the customer was in hospital
  - the customer was travelling outside the Kingdom
  - the reporting authority delayed the accident report
- Other reasons are not accepted, for example: [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
  - being busy at work
  - forgetting
  - not knowing the deadline

## 11. Required documents

- Every claim needs all of the following, for the vehicle being claimed for: [SAMA Instructions for Motor Insurance Claims' Settlement, circular 14 May 2023]
  - the accident report (Najm or traffic police)
  - the vehicle registration
  - a copy of the driving licence
  - photos of the damage
  - a damage assessment report (for example Taqdeer)
- For an own-damage claim these are the policyholder's vehicle registration, damage assessment and damage photos, the licence copy of the driver at the time, and the accident report. For a third-party claim they are the claimant's vehicle registration and damage assessment, the at-fault driver's licence copy, the accident report, and photos of the claimant's vehicle damage. [SAMA Instructions for Motor Insurance Claims' Settlement, circular 14 May 2023]
- If any is missing, the decision is need info and no payout is computed. A garage quote does not replace the damage assessment report. If two damage assessment values contradict each other, the decision is also need info. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]

## 12. Order of application

- Rules are checked in this order, and the first one that leads to a decision decides: out of scope (2) → policy not in force (3) → third-party exclusions (6) → own damage under TPL (3, 9) → own-damage exclusions (8) → reporting window (10) → repair at or below the full deductible, when the damage assessment is present and not contradicted (7) → required documents (11) → payout (5 or 7). [insurer-specific (illustrative)]

## 13. Service standards and other benefits

- If documents are missing, the insurer tells individual customers within 3 working days. [SAMA Instructions for Motor Insurance Claims' Settlement, circular 14 May 2023]
- Claims below SAR 2,000 are settled within 5 working days; other claims within 15 days. [SAMA Instructions for Motor Insurance Claims' Settlement, circular 14 May 2023]
- A total-loss report is issued within 7 working days of complete documents. [SAMA Instructions for Motor Insurance Claims' Settlement, circular 14 May 2023]
- Claim statuses: Incomplete, Outstanding, Settled, Rejected. [SAMA Instructions for Motor Insurance Claims' Settlement, circular 14 May 2023]
- Towing is covered per claim up to SAR 500 inside a city and SAR 1,000 outside a city. [CMIR Policy Schedule, per-claim coverage limit (insurer may agree higher)]
- A replacement car is not part of this policy. It is an optional add-on that needs the insurer's prior approval. [insurer-specific (illustrative)]
